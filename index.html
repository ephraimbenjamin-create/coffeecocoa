Why they matter

  • Cocoa: Rich in flavanols that support cardiovascular health and mood
  • A natural source of magnesium, iron, and antioxidants
  • Contains theobromine — a gentle, longer-lasting stimulant
  • Forms the base of chocolate — one of the world's most traded commodities
  • Coffee: The world's most widely consumed psychoactive beverage
  • Linked to improved cognitive function and alertness
  • A significant source of dietary antioxidants in many populations
  • Supports millions of smallholder farmers in tropical regions

Cocoa Origins

West Africa
Ghana & Côte d'Ivoire
Produces over 60% of the world's cocoa. Deep red soils and equatorial climate yield full-bodied, classic chocolate flavor with gentle earthiness.
South America
Ecuador & Peru
Home of the prized Nacional and Arriba varieties. Known for floral, fruity notes — often called the finest cacao in the world.
Southeast Asia
Indonesia & PNG
Offers a unique earthy, smoky character. Indonesia is among the top three producers globally, with strong bulk cocoa exports.
Caribbean
Trinidad & Venezuela
Cradle of the rare Criollo bean — the "prince of cacao." Delicate, complex and low in bitterness, used in premium chocolates worldwide.

Coffee Origins

East Africa
Ethiopia & Kenya
Birthplace of coffee. Ethiopian beans burst with jasmine, blueberry and citrus. Kenyan AA is celebrated for its wine-like brightness and acidity.
Central America
Guatemala & Colombia
High altitudes and volcanic soil produce balanced, sweet coffees. Colombian beans are among the most recognized and beloved globally.
South America
Brazil
The world's largest producer. Brazilian beans offer nutty, chocolatey, low-acid profiles — ideal for espresso blends and everyday drinking.
Southeast Asia
Sumatra & Vietnam
Sumatran coffee is bold, earthy and full-bodied. Vietnam dominates Robusta production, supplying much of the global instant coffee market.

Cocoa Flavor Notes

Dark chocolate Caramel Raspberry Earthy Hazelnut Dried fruit Tobacco Honey Red berries Mushroom Vanilla Floral

Cocoa's flavor spectrum is shaped by bean variety, fermentation time, and roast level. Criollo beans tend toward delicate fruit and floral notes; Forastero is robust and earthy; Trinitario sits somewhere in between.

Coffee Flavor Notes

Blueberry Toffee Cedar Jasmine Brown sugar Dark roast Citrus zest Chocolate Smoke Stone fruit Walnut Spice

Coffee's flavor complexity is unmatched — over 1,000 aromatic compounds identified. Light roasts preserve origin character; dark roasts develop caramelized depth. Processing method — washed, natural, or honey — dramatically shifts the cup's character.

The taste of terroir

Like fine wine, both cocoa and coffee express the character of their environment — the soil mineral content, altitude, rainfall, and microclimate all leave a fingerprint in the final cup or bar. This is why single-origin products are treasured: each one tells a specific place's story.

From pod to chocolate

1
Harvesting
Cacao pods are hand-cut from the trunk using machetes. Only ripe pods are selected — harvest twice yearly.
2
Fermentation
Seeds and pulp ferment in wooden boxes for 5–8 days. This step develops precursor flavor compounds.
3
Drying
Beans are sun-dried for 1–2 weeks to reduce moisture and halt fermentation. Even drying is essential.
4
Roasting
Roasters apply precisely controlled heat to develop chocolate flavors from the fermented bean.
5
Conching
Liquid chocolate is mixed and aerated for hours or days, refining texture and mellowing harsh notes.

From cherry to cup

1
Harvesting
Coffee cherries are picked by hand (selective picking) or by strip-harvesting entire branches at once.
2
Processing
Washed, natural, or honey — the method of removing the cherry fruit transforms the seed's flavor profile.
3
Drying & milling
Green beans are dried to ~11% moisture, then milled to remove the parchment layer before grading.
4
Roasting
Green beans are roasted from light to dark, triggering the Maillard reaction that creates hundreds of new flavor compounds.
5
Brewing
Hot water extracts soluble compounds from ground coffee. Method, grind size, and water temperature all shape the cup.
